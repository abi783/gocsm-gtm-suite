Part 1 · Secret #2 in every variant

The 5 Churn SignalsEach one: what it is → why it predicts → lead time → manual GHL spot-check → thresholds.

The signals fire in sequence — adoption first, payment last

Lead time before cancellation. The earlier signals buy you the most room to intervene.

1

Declining feature adoption

Lead time · 30–60 days · strongest predictor

Breadth of usage shrinks — workflows + calendars + campaigns down to just Conversations. 5+ features retain ~92–96% vs ~60–75% for 1–2.

🟡 ≤2 core features, or one abandoned this month
🔴 −2 features in 60d, or no 2nd feature by Day 30

The med spa that ran calendars + reactivation campaigns — then by May used GHL as an inbox. By June: "remind me what we're paying for?"

2

Usage & spend decline

Lead time · 30–45 days · the velocity drop

Same features, falling volume — fewer messages, fewer leads, wallet spend trending down. "I don't see results" is the #1 reason locals fire agencies.

🟡 20–30% decline in any core metric MoM
🔴 50%+ decline, or wallet near zero

The gym workflow: 400 SMS in Jan, 220 Feb, 90 Mar. The wallet chart screamed for 60 days before "we're pausing — money's tight."

3

Stale assets / ghost account

Lead time · 45–90 days · longest fuse, easiest to miss

Nothing new created. Old automations keep firing so dashboards look "active" — but no human is investing in the account or the relationship.

🟡 No new/modified asset in 30 days
🔴 Nothing in 60d+ AND no work scheduled

The realtor's 2024 listing-alert workflow still fired daily. No new campaign since November. "I don't think anyone's actually working on my account."

4

Sentiment drop & silence

Lead time · 14–45 days · a late-stage signal

NPS falls, replies get terse — or go silent. Only 1 in 26 unhappy customers complains; the rest just churn. Absence of complaints ≠ satisfaction.

🟡 Passive NPS, slow replies, unresolved complaint
🔴 Detractor + any behavior signal, or 30d silence

The dentist complained twice in October, got it fixed, never complained again — never replied again either. Cancelled in January.

5

Payment friction + renewal proximity — the compound signal

Lead time · 7–30 days (renewal window opens at 90)

Failed payments, expiring cards, or an approaching renewal — landing on an account already unhealthy on signals 1–4. Involuntary churn is 20–40% of all churn, and the most fixable: dunning recovers 40–60%+. But a failed payment on a low-health account 30 days from renewal is a cancellation in progress.

🟡 One recovered failure, card expiring <30d, renewal <90d on a steady account
🔴 Repeated failures, any downgrade, or renewal <60d + health <60

The contractor's card expired in November — three silent retries, account suspended, no call. By January he'd signed with a competitor: "I figured you didn't notice I was gone."

Part 2 · Secret #1 in every variant

The Client Health ScoreNo single metric predicts churn — weighted composites do. Weights reflect predictive power, not ease of measurement.

The formula

Health = Adoption 40 + Revenue 30 + Sentiment 20 + Login 10.

Per-dimension rubric · score each 0–100

  • Adoption (40) · 5+ feat = 100 · 3–4 = 70 · 2 = 40 · 1 = 20 · declining −20
  • Revenue (30) · growing = 100 · flat = 70 · −20–30% = 40 · failed pay = 10
  • Sentiment (20) · promoter = 100 · passive = 60 · no response = 50 · detractor = 10
  • Login (10) · weekly = 100 · monthly = 60 · 30d dark = 0

Part 3 · Secret #3 support · Variant C centerpiece

The 90-Day Retention Playbook40–60% of cancellations happen in the first 90 days. Your client doesn't buy GHL — they buy the phone ringing.

Days 0–30 · Activation

  • Day 0–3: kickoff, define the one outcome, set expectations
  • Day 7: first campaign live
  • Day 10–14: first lead delivered — celebrate loudly
  • Target: ≥60% hit first value by Day 30

Days 31–60 · Habit

  • Add 2nd & 3rd feature — multi-feature ≈ 70% higher retention
  • Danger window: Day 45–75 usage drop = top predictor
  • Day 60: mini-QBR in their language — leads, not impressions

Days 61–90 · Proof

  • Day 90 QBR: leads × bookings × revenue vs fee
  • "You paid $297 — the system booked 41 appointments"
  • Healthy → expansion. At-risk → re-onboard, not discount.
The automated layer (GoCSM triggers): inactivity → re-engagement · feature gap at Day 30 → onboarding nudge · failed payment → dunning (recovers 40–60%+) · renewal T-90/60/30 + low health → human outreach. Win-back: highest response in the first 30–60 days post-cancel, lead with the fixed issue, not a discount.

Part 6 · Seasoning — one story per point, never two

The GHL-audience story bankDocumented agency-client failure patterns that make every abstract point land.

The vanity-report cancellation

"Impressions up, but my phone isn't ringing." #1 documented reason locals fire agencies. → Report in leads/bookings/revenue.

The quiet-phase breakup

Breakups start with slower replies, fewer approvals, less trust — not a blow-up. → Signals 3 + 4.

The "fine last month" client

Every owner has one. → 1-in-26; sentiment is late, behavior leaks first.

The 3am cancellation email

The visceral shared moment. → Opens Variant A.

The expectations time-bomb

Over-promised at sale, under-delivered by Day 30. → Day 0–3 kickoff.

The communication tax

"Can't reply in 48h — do they even care?" → Response-time as a sentiment proxy.

The January/December effect

Year-end budget cuts cluster churn; gyms churn into January. → Renewal risk isn't evenly spread.

The "I figured you didn't notice" contractor

The most damning line a churned client can say. → The case for automated payment monitoring.
